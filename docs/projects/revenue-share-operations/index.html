Why it mattered

Revenue-share operations connect source reports, merchant relationships, partner calculations, and payments. Leadership needs to understand both what the business earns and how the underlying activity supports that result.

The problem

Separate files and partner-specific calculations make recurring reporting difficult to follow. A mismatch can originate in source data, merchant mapping, calculation rules, or payment activity; those differences need to remain traceable.

My role

I own the end-to-end operational workflow and reporting development, including recurring reconciliation and investigation of revenue-share discrepancies. I coordinate with internal stakeholders to clarify rules and resolve exceptions.

The approach

  1. Centralized merchant mappings and partner setup so recurring calculations use consistent reference data.
  2. Brought monthly revenue, revenue-share calculations, partner statements, reconciliation, and payout tracking into a connected workbook.
  3. Built validation and exception views to investigate differences and compare revised outputs with the legacy process.
  4. Developed reporting for different audiences and documented recurring steps to make the process easier to maintain.
What changed
  • Centralized the inputs and reporting needed to follow revenue-share activity through the workflow.
  • Made merchant mapping, partner logic, reconciliation, and payout status available within a connected tracking process.
  • Created a structure for recurring exception investigation and reporting; the contract work is ongoing.

What I built

A redesigned revenue-share tracker with connected reference data, calculation logic, reconciliation, payout tracking, and partner and management reporting.
